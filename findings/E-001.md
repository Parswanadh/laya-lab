## 2. What the harness is

`experiments/harness/run.py --preset baseline-repro --device cuda --checkpoint models/multilingual`
is the whole interface. It builds needle-in-haystack documents, runs the shipped checkpoint through
`laya.load`/`Agent.predict`, and writes raw per-item JSONL. It takes the GPU lock itself
(non-blocking `flock` on `.gpu.lock`) and refuses to start when free RAM/VRAM is below the gate. It
never modifies `fork/`.

Parameterised by `--pads`, `--positions` (needle position as a fraction of the filler),
`--max-lens`, `--head-max-lens`, `--n`, `--seed`, `--device`, `--languages`. `default` in a
max-len list means *do not override*, i.e. the value the checkpoint ships.

Per item it records: item id, label, language, template, prediction, probability vector, needle
position, pad, `state_tokens_full`/`state_tokens_kept`/`input_tokens`, **how many request tokens
survived truncation**, latency, and `doc_sha256`. The full document text is not stored; it is
regenerated exactly from `(pool, seed, item_id, pad, position)` and the hash is re-derived by
`checks.py` to prove it.

Pools: `balanced-v1` (3 labels × 9 languages × 3 templates, one shared numeric slot pool) and
`filler-v1` (30 office-logistics sentences, no digits, no support-domain vocabulary). The
filler/needle disjointness check passes (`content_word_overlap: []`, `stem_hits: []`). The balanced
item draw fixes both marginals exactly — label `n//3`, language `n//9` — so a constant answer
cannot exceed ~1/3 on any cell and language carries no label signal.

## 5. Result — label-balanced arm, n=20 per cell (measured)

Pool `balanced-v1`, seed 20240924, needle at the end of the document (position 1.00),
`head_max_len` shipped (256). Chance references on this pool: random over the 4 options 0.25,
random over the 3 gold labels 0.333, majority class 0.35 (the draw is 7 billing / 7 technical /
6 sales).

| pad (filler tokens) | max_len | n | correct | accuracy | 95% CI | macro-F1 | modal prediction (share) | distinct predictions | request tokens kept (median) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | default (1024) | 20 | 19 | **0.950** | [0.85, 1.00] | 0.947 | billing (0.40) | 3 | 18/18 |
| 0 | 8192 | 20 | 19 | **0.950** | [0.85, 1.00] | 0.947 | billing (0.40) | 3 | 18/18 |
| 1000 | default (1024) | 20 | 7 | **0.350** | [0.15, 0.55] | 0.173 | technical (1.00) | 1 | 0/18 |
| 1000 | 8192 | 20 | 14 | 0.700 | [0.50, 0.90] | 0.692 | technical (0.50) | 3 | 18/18 |
| 2000 | default (1024) | 20 | 7 | **0.350** | [0.15, 0.55] | 0.173 | technical (1.00) | 1 | 0/18 |
| 2000 | 8192 | 20 | 14 | 0.700 | [0.50, 0.90] | 0.698 | technical (0.65) | 3 | 18/18 |
| 4000 | default (1024) | 20 | 6 | **0.300** | [0.10, 0.50] | 0.154 | technical (0.95) | 2 | 0/18 |
| 4000 | 8192 | 20 | 11 | 0.550 | [0.35, 0.75] | 0.445 | technical (0.80) | 2 | 18/18 |
| 7000 | default (1024) | 20 | 7 | **0.350** | [0.15, 0.55] | 0.173 | technical (1.00) | 1 | 0/18 |
| 7000 | 8192 | 20 | 8 | 0.400 | [0.20, 0.60] | 0.269 | technical (0.85) | 3 | 18/18 |

Column meanings: `distinct predictions` is the number of different answers the model gave across
the 20 items of the cell (1 = every item got the same answer); `request tokens kept` is the median
number of needle-request tokens still inside the model's window divided by the median request
length. `max_len_effective` is 1024 for both `default` rows, since that is what the checkpoint
ships.

**Collapse.** At the shipped default, **every cell with pad > 0 answered `technical` for all 20
items** — `billing → technical:7`, `sales → technical:6`, `technical → technical:7` — and not one
request token survived truncation. Accuracy 0.35 is exactly the share of `technical` in the draw
(7/20). The modal probability for `technical` in those cells is 0.46–0.98 (median ≈ 0.78–0.81): the
model is *confident* about an answer that does not depend on the document. Because each item in
this arm has its own seeded filler, the 20 probability vectors are not byte-identical (20 distinct
vectors), but the **decision** is constant (1 distinct prediction).

**Recovery at 8192.** With `max_len=8192` no cell is truncated (`request tokens kept` = 18/18 for
every item), and accuracy rises from 0.35 to 0.70 at pads 1000 and 2000, 0.55 at 4000, and 0.40 at
7000. The model keeps a strong `technical` bias even when it can see the needle, which caps the
ceiling on this pool.

## 6. Result — upstream's published numbers, and what is inside them

*From upstream artifact* `fork/research/results/long_context_multilingual.json` (Apple MPS, their
20-request pool, n=20/cell). Re-derived here from their raw `cases`, not from their prose:

| pad | max_len=1024 | max_len=8192 |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.95 (19/20) | 0.95 (19/20) |
| 1000 | 0.65 (13/20) | 0.80 (16/20) |
| 2000 | **0.35 (7/20)** | 0.85 (17/20) |
| 4000 | **0.35 (7/20)** | 0.90 (18/20) |
| 7000 | **0.35 (7/20)** | 0.40 (8/20) |

Two facts about their raw rows, which matter for how the 0.35 is read:

1. At pad ≥ 2000 with `max_len=1024`, **all 20 predictions are `technical`** and there is **exactly
   one distinct probability vector** across the 20 items — the model returned byte-identical output
   for 20 different requests.
2. Their gold distribution is **9 billing / 7 technical / 4 sales**, so a constant `technical`
   answer scores exactly **7/20 = 0.35** and the majority class is 0.45. Their 0.35 is therefore
   the rate of a constant answer, not a chance level (chance over 3 answerable labels is 0.333).

The same constant-answer rate is what we measure at the shipped default on a *balanced* pool
(0.35 = the 7/20 technical share of our draw). The agreement in the number is a coincidence of two
different label mixes; the agreement in the **mechanism** — zero request tokens retained, one
constant answer — is the substantive reproduction.

## 7. Mechanism: what the 1024-token window does to the document

Measured from the harness diagnostics (the keep-length is taken from the library's own
`build_sequence`, and checked against the documented rule in every row: `trunc_rule_ok` is true for
all 200 rows). With `max_len=1024` and `head_max_len=256` the prompt head measures **45 tokens**, so
the state window is `1024 − 45 − 1 = 978` tokens.

* Balanced arm: the filler is built to an *exact* token budget, so at pad=1000 the request starts
  at token 1000 > 978 and **nothing of it survives** — hence the total collapse at pad=1000.
* Upstream arm (derived, not yet measured with the model): upstream's filler is one sentence
  repeated `round(pad / 43)` times. Because BPE merges across the repetition boundary, the real
  cost per repetition is ≈ 42 tokens, so at pad=1000 the composed state is only 983–996 tokens and
  the window still contains **6–7 tokens of each request**; at pad=2000 the state is 1991–2004
  tokens and **0 request tokens survive**.

Derived per-cell truncation for the 20 upstream requests at `max_len=1024` (real tokenizer,
`room = 978`; computed by `DocBuilder.diagnose`, no model involved):

| pad | state tokens (min–max) | request tokens (min–max) | request tokens kept (min–max) | rows fully kept | rows with none kept |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 12–25 | 12–25 | 12–25 | 20/20 | 0/20 |
| 1000 | 983–996 | 12–25 | **6–7** | 0/20 | 0/20 |
| 2000 | 1991–2004 | 12–25 | **0–0** | 0/20 | 20/20 |

That is exactly the shape of upstream's measured accuracy: 0.65 at pad=1000 (roughly a third of
each request still visible — enough for the label-bearing first words, not enough to be reliable),
then a hard floor from pad=2000 on. This is a *derived* prediction of their curve from the
truncation rule; the upstream-faithful arm is the measurement that tests it (`tables.py` prints
`median_request_tokens_kept` per cell for exactly this comparison).

**Pre-registered prediction for the position sweep** (written before that run started, so the run
either confirms it or does not). At pad=4000 with `max_len=1024` the window is the first 978
filler tokens, and the needle starts at `round(position × 4000)`:

| needle position | needle starts at token | inside the 978-token window? | predicted accuracy |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.00 | 0 | yes (whole needle) | high (~pad-0 level) |
| 0.25 | 1000 | no | at chance = the constant-answer share |
| 0.50 | 2000 | no | at chance |
| 0.75 | 3000 | no | at chance |
| 1.00 | 4000 | no | at chance |

With `max_len=8192` (document ≈ 4022 tokens, nothing truncated) the same five positions should all
be readable, with a possible middle-position dip from attention dilution. If the 1024 rows instead
degrade smoothly with position, the "evidence is truncated away" explanation is wrong and this
finding must say so.

Two things these numbers show, both expected: (a) the 1024 rows cost the same regardless of the
document, because the document is cut to the same 1024 tokens — the collapse is free; (b) latency
grows with tokens read, from 0.02 s at 64 tokens to 0.56 s at 7 068 tokens. Upstream measured

sets are not comparable — only the within-machine curve is.

## 9. What is measured to reproduce, and what is not

Reportable, from the tables above (n=20/cell, this machine):

1. **The collapse reproduces.** At the shipped `max_len=1024`, every document ≥ 1000 tokens is
   answered with one constant label, no request token survives, and accuracy equals that label's
   share of a balanced draw (0.30–0.35 across cells).
2. **The exact 0.35 reproduces, for a stated reason.** Upstream's 0.35 is the constant-`technical`
   rate on a 9/7/4 pool; ours is the constant-`technical` rate on a 7/7/6 draw. Both are 7/20.
3. **Raising `max_len` to 8192 restores the input dependence**, but on the balanced pool it reaches
   0.55–0.70, **not** upstream's 0.85–0.90. The gap is not truncation: at 8192 the whole request is
   present in every cell. Candidate explanations that this data can separate (a harder balanced
   pool with nine languages and fresh templates; a `technical` bias that costs accuracy even when
   the needle is read; platform/precision differences MPS-fp16 vs CUDA-bf16) are *not* separated by
   this run alone — that is what the upstream-faithful arm (same pool, same texts) is for.
4. **At pad=7000 the model fails even with full visibility** (0.40 at 8192, upstream also 0.40).
   This is dilution, not truncation, and it is a separate defect from the one this program is
   chasing.

## 10. What this does not claim

* Synthetic needle-in-haystack only. The filler is repetitive and unlike real documents
  (`protocol.md` §10); nothing here transfers to real long-document behaviour.
* n=20 per cell. The 95% CI at n=20 is ±0.20, so no two cells closer than ~0.4 apart are
  distinguishable in this table (protocol §3). **IN FLIGHT:** the n=200 runs.
* One seed (20240924) for the item draw. Seed spread is not measured (protocol §6).
* `L ∈ {0, 1000, 2000, 4000, 7000}` only. `L = 8192/16000/32000` was not run: the encoder's
  `max_position_embeddings` is 8192 and the baseline question is 1024-vs-8192, not arms.
* `predict_long` (upstream PR #363) is not in this checkout (`fork` @ `9fbb1ebecf33`), so
  protocol §7 baseline 6 is unavailable here.
* Latency includes tokenization and is single-item, single-process, GPU-lock-held; the box also
  runs other agents' CPU work, so treat p95 as an upper bound.

## 11. Incidents

1. **A cgroup cap of 1 GB OOM-killed the first attempt at the real run** (my launcher, before the
   model finished loading; `systemd` logged `Failed with result 'oom-kill'`). The harness's own
   preflight had passed because it gates on *host* free memory, not on the container cap. Fixed by
   launching the wrapper with `MemoryMax=4G` and recording `LAYA_MEMORY_CAP` in the manifest.
   Nothing was measured in that attempt.
2. **GPU contention.** A second agent held `.gpu.lock` for the first ~13 minutes. The harness
   refused to start (exit 2) rather than run concurrently, as designed; a bounded waiter acquired
   the lock later. The reported run held the lock for its whole sweep (`gpu_lock.held: true`).
3. **`protocol.md` did not exist when this task started** (it appeared while the harness was being
   built). The harness was built to the issue text and then checked against the frozen protocol;
   the gaps found (language axis incl. `zh`, macro-F1, random baselines, position-only oracle,
   ≥3 warm-up passes, peak VRAM) were closed and are marked in the manifest's `protocol` block.
