## 1. What was wrong with the first arm 3 (not re-derived)

Recorded in `progress.md` (2026-09-25, H5 arm3) and ledger **L-037**: `arm3_xattn` scored
**0.225–0.290 at every cell including L0** — below chance on a 64-token input with no long-range
problem in it, so the arm did not learn the task. From `runs/arm3_xattn/seed0/training.json` it
trained a **randomly initialised** `head.layers.*.cross_attn.*` where arm 2 trained the
**pre-trained** `head.layers.*.self_attn.*`, under an identical recipe (lr 5e-4, 12 epochs, 4848
steps). Arm 2 started from the shipped solution, arm 3 from noise. That comparison cannot separate
"cross-attention is worse" from "a from-scratch module under a recipe tuned for fine-tuning did not
converge", and it is not evidence about the architecture.

## 2. The arm as built

| arm | aggregation | initialisation | schedule |
|---|---|---|---|
| `arm1_frozen` | shipped self-attention | shipped checkpoint | none |
| `arm2_shipped_init` | shipped self-attention | shipped head, then trained | 12 epochs, lr 5e-4 (existing record) |
| `arm3_xattn` | cross-attention **replaces** self-attention | random | 12 epochs, lr 5e-4 (failed to train, L-037) |
| **`arm3r_residual`** | shipped self-attention **plus a parallel cross-attention branch** | shipped head + **zero-initialised branch** | **40 epochs**, lr 5e-4 |
| **`arm2long_shipped_init`** | shipped self-attention | shipped head, then trained | **40 epochs**, lr 5e-4 |

`head_kind="self_attention_plus_cross"` (`worktrees/h5/laya/common.py`, commit `99020ba`) keeps the
`nn.TransformerEncoder` the checkpoint was trained with and adds a `ResidualCrossAttention` branch
that reads the **same** input (encoder output + `type_emb`), cross-attends from the decision tokens
to the **state tokens only**, and **adds** its output to the decision positions:

```
h = self_attention(h_in)                       # the shipped path, untouched
delta = cross.out_proj(cross.branch(h_in[:dmax], h_in, kv_pad))   # out_proj == 0 at init
h[:, :dmax] += delta
```

Three properties are built in rather than argued:

1. **Identity at step 0.** `out_proj.weight` and `out_proj.bias` are zero-initialised, so `delta` is
   exactly `0` and `x + 0.0 = x` bitwise in IEEE-754. `arm3r_residual` is therefore *bit-for-bit*
   `arm2_shipped_init` before the first optimiser step — **asserted** over all 1600 evaluation items
   by `step0.py`, not assumed, with a non-zero-branch control so that a branch which is simply not
   connected cannot pass the assertion (§3).
2. **The pre-trained self-attention stage is kept exactly.** The branch has its **own** head count
   (`cross_nhead=4`); the self-attention stage keeps the shipped geometry (12 heads, `d//64`). Head
   count changes no tensor's *shape*, so sharing one `nhead` between the two stages would load the
   pre-trained weights without error and then compute a different function — the offline selftest
   caught exactly that during construction, and `build_arm_model` now asserts the self-attention head
   count equals the shipped one.
3. **The intervention is measured, not assumed.** `train.py` records, every epoch: the branch's
   output-projection norm, and the branch's contribution to the logits (one fixed batch scored with
   the branch as trained and again with `out_proj` re-zeroed). Both start at `0` and must move, or
   the arm is invalid, not null (`protocol.md` §10).

**Recipe.** Unchanged from arm 2 except the epoch count: AdamW, weight decay 0.01, token budget
12288, max batch 8, `warmup_frac=0.05`, cosine decay to zero, bf16 autocast over **fp32 master
weights**, encoder frozen, uniform training grid (1008 items), same seed-0 shuffle. The only other
difference is the branch's own learning rate, chosen by a short probe and reported in §4.

**A confound, stated up front.** `arm3r_residual` keeps arm 2's head *and* adds a cross-attention
stage, so it carries strictly more trainable parameters: **29,537,281 vs arm 2's 14,770,945**
(+14,766,336, **+99.97 %**, from `arms.analytic_parameter_table()`, d=768 — the added branch is a
`CrossAttentionHead` plus its output projection). Unlike the first arm 3 it is **not**
parameter-matched, and a gain is attributable to *the added branch* — capacity included — not to a
parameter-matched architectural substitution. Two consequences, fixed in advance: (i) the
`arm3r_residual_ablated` control in §4.3 measures how much of arm3r's score the branch itself
causes; (ii) **if arm3r wins, a capacity-matched control (arm 2 plus two extra self-attention
layers) is required before any architectural claim**, and is run before the finding is closed. If
arm3r does not win, extra capacity that does not help is part of the negative result rather than a
threat to it.

## 3. Step-0 identity assertion

`experiments/h5-adapter/step0_identity.json` · `env/venv/bin/python experiments/h5-adapter/step0.py`
(GPU lock held, `flock -n .gpu.lock`; 300 s; fork worktree `99020ba`)

| # | check | n | bitwise-identical logits | max \|Δlogit\| | items with a different prediction |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `arm3r == arm2`, shipped init, **all 11 cells** | 2200 | **yes** | **0.0** | **0** |
| 2 | `arm2`'s **trained** seed-0 head loaded into both architectures, 3 control cells | 600 | **yes** | **0.0** | **0** |
| 3 | liveness: a non-zero branch (1e-3) must **not** equal arm2, `L7000-p100` | 200 | no (moves) | 0.03125 | 0 (diagnostic) |

`out_proj` weight and bias absolute sums after construction: **0.0 / 0.0**. Trainability of the
branch on a real batch: `out_proj.weight` gradient sum **50.23**, bias **0.0966**, and all **24**
inner tensors exactly **0.0** — the expected consequence of `dL/dz = Wᵀ dL/dδ = 0` at `W = 0`, not a
defect: the branch first learns a linear read-out of its random features and only then adapts them.
**Verdict: `VALID`.**

Three notes on the assertion itself, because they are what make it worth something:

* **The gate is on the logits, not the argmax.** The first run of this script exited 1 with
  `INVALID` because the liveness check additionally required a *prediction* to flip: at a probe
  scale of 1e-3 the logits did move (one bf16 ULP) but no argmax changed on 200 items. "Is the
  branch wired in?" and "is this perturbation big enough to change an answer?" are different
  questions; the check now gates on the first and reports the second as a diagnostic. (Diagnosed by
  the orchestrator from the artifact; recorded as ledger L-045/L-046.)
* **Identity alone is satisfiable by dead code**, which is why check 3 exists: a branch that is not
  connected passes checks 1 and 2 trivially. Checks 1+2 and check 3 together are the real assertion.
* A third, larger probe (3e-2) was run for *reach* and reported in the artifact
  (`reach.prediction_flips`): a random projection that large still changes **no** answer, so what
  the branch contributes has to be *learned* — consistent with the trained branch's contribution
  growing to 0.19 logits in two epochs of the LR probe (§4.5).

**A defect this assertion caught before the run.** The first construction of the arm passed
`nhead=4` to *both* stages, so the self-attention stage would have had 4 heads where the shipped
head has 12. Head count changes no tensor's *shape*, so the pre-trained weights would have loaded
without error and then computed a different function — the arm would have kept the pre-trained head
in name only, and `arm3r ≡ arm2` would have been false for a reason nothing else reports. The
offline selftest caught it (max |Δlogit| 0.008 on the tiny model); the branch now has its own
`cross_nhead`, and `build_arm_model` asserts the self-attention head count equals the shipped one.

### 3.1 Branch learning rate (probe, `probe_arm3r.json`)

The rest of the recipe stays at arm 2's `lr = 5e-4` — the difference between the arms has to stay
the architecture — and only the branch's rate varies. Three epochs on the real 1008-item training
set, cached features, identical batching and schedule shape:

| `lr_cross` | train loss / epoch | train acc / epoch | branch \|Δlogit\| / epoch | ‖W_out‖_F / epoch |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5e-4 (= main) | 1.3660 → 1.3266 → **1.3040** | 0.331 / 0.339 / **0.349** | 0.051 / 0.090 / 0.082 | 5.4 / 6.6 / 6.7 |
| **3e-3 (chosen)** | 1.3651 → 1.3172 → **1.3026** | 0.341 / 0.343 / **0.375** | 0.010 / 0.204 / 0.193 | 25.9 / 32.6 / 32.5 |
| 1e-2 | 1.3639 → 1.3311 → 1.3248 | 0.333 / 0.341 / 0.330 | 0.129 / 0.711 / 0.657 | 80.8 / 101.7 / 102.4 |

**Chosen: `lr_cross = 3e-3`, 6× the main rate.** It has the lowest final loss *and* the highest final
train accuracy of the three; 1e-2 moves the branch 3.4× further (‖W‖ 102 vs 32, contribution 0.66 vs
0.19) and is *worse* on both (loss 1.3248 vs 1.3026, accuracy 0.330 vs 0.375) — it buys branch
magnitude by degrading the head it was added to. 5e-4 moves the branch least (‖W‖ 6.7). The choice
is recorded in `runs/arm3r_residual/seed0/training.json` as `lr_cross`.

## 4. Pre-registered analysis plan (written before training)

Fixed now so that it cannot be chosen after seeing the numbers:

1. **Primary cell** `L7000-p100`, n=200, label-balanced, position-only oracle **0.2500**. Reported
   against **arm2 = 0.455** (its record, 12 epochs) **and** against the schedule-matched
   `arm2long_shipped_init` trained here. `L4000-p050` (arm2 = 0.500) and `L0` (arm2 = 0.620) are the
   controls; `L0` has no long-range problem, so an arm that loses there has not learned the task.
2. **Verdict rule.** The architectural claim requires arm3r > arm2long at the primary cell with a
   paired McNemar test surviving Holm–Bonferroni over the comparison family, *and* the difference
   outside the seed spread, *and* arm3r above the position-only oracle. Anything else is reported as
   arm 3 **not** beating arm 2 — in that case the honest conclusion is *"the fix is adaptation, not
   design"*, which issue #6 pre-registered as a real possible outcome.
3. **A gain counts only if the branch caused it.** The `arm3r_residual_ablated` control (the trained
   arm with `out_proj` re-zeroed after training) measures how much of arm3r's score is the branch;
   arm2long measures how much is the longer schedule.
4. **Convergence is reported, not asserted.** Per-epoch loss and train accuracy for both arms over
   the full schedule, plus the change over the last five epochs. If the loss is still falling at the
   end, the run is reported as **not converged** and the comparison is labelled schedule-limited.
5. **Branch LR.** Probed at three rates on the real training data with the rest of the recipe fixed;
   the choice, the curves, and the reason are reported (`probe_arm3r.json`).

## 6. The control that decides what the arm means: `arm3r_residual_ablated`

`arm3r_residual_ablated` is the **same trained checkpoint with `out_proj` re-zeroed after
training** — the added branch switched off, everything else identical
(`eval.py --zero-branch`; `predictions/arm3r_residual_ablated-seed0.jsonl`). It is the cheapest
control that should not help if the branch contributes nothing, and it is decisive here:

| cell | arm3r | ablated | Δ | items whose prediction changed | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L0 | 0.595 | 0.595 | 0.0 | **0 / 200** | 1.0 |
| L4000-p000 | 0.715 | 0.715 | 0.0 | 2 / 200 | 1.0 |
| L4000-p025 | 0.520 | 0.525 | −0.5 | 5 / 200 | 1.0 |
| L4000-p050 | 0.590 | 0.590 | 0.0 | 2 / 200 | 1.0 |
| L4000-p075 | 0.555 | 0.565 | −1.0 | 2 / 200 | 0.5 |
| L4000-p100 | 0.560 | 0.560 | 0.0 | 2 / 200 | 1.0 |
| L7000-p000 | 0.680 | 0.680 | 0.0 | 2 / 200 | 1.0 |
| L7000-p100 | 0.485 | 0.500 | −1.5 | **3 / 200** | 0.25 |

**Zeroing the trained branch changes at most 2.5 % of decisions at any cell, and 0 at `L0`.** The
branch is live during training (its contribution grows to ≈0.5 logits, ‖W_out‖ 7 → 142) but at
**inference the head does not use it**. It follows that:

* The cells where arm3r does win significantly — `L4000-p000` (+18.5 pp vs arm2long, Holm 3.5e-05)
  and `L7000-p000` (+14.0 pp, Holm 0.015) — are **not** attributable to the added cross-attention.
  The ablated twin wins those same cells by the same margin (vs arm2: +12.5 pp, Holm 0.028 and
  +9.5 pp, Holm 0.032). What differs from the plain head is the **training trajectory**, not the
  branch's output.
* The primary-cell difference and the p000-cell gains are therefore evidence about *optimisation*,
  not about the architecture. The cross-attention branch, as built and trained here, is an
  inference-inert module with a training-time side effect.

**And the comparison is not trajectory-controlled even in principle.** `arm3r_residual` and
`arm2long_shipped_init` share a seed and a data order, but the branch consumes dropout RNG every
step, so the two runs are different dropout draws from step 1 onward. A +7.5 pp single-seed gap
between two such runs is not separable from trajectory noise without extra seeds — which is exactly
why the ablation, not the accuracy gap, is the load-bearing measurement in this section.

## 7. Convergence

Both arms ran the identical schedule: 40 epochs, 16 160 steps, AdamW, lr 5e-4, cosine to zero,
`warmup_frac` 0.05, token budget 12 288, uniform 1008-item grid. `arm3r_residual` additionally ran
the branch at `lr_cross` 3e-3.

| | train loss | train acc | last-5-epoch loss | held-out `L7000-p100` (in-training probe, n=200) | time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| arm2 (12 ep, existing record) | 1.4136 → 1.2585 | 0.344 → 0.383 | falling | — | 1089 s |
| **arm2long (40 ep)** | 1.4417 → **1.1134** | 0.337 → **0.4861** | 1.1413 → 1.1134 (falling) | 0.445 / 0.425 / 0.420 / 0.450 / **0.410** at ep 7/15/23/31/39 | 3337 s |
| **arm3r (40 ep)** | 1.4396 → **1.1588** | 0.337 → **0.4722** | 1.1823 → 1.1588 (falling) | 0.405 / 0.385 / 0.490 / 0.500 / **0.490** | 3968 s |

**Convergence, stated as measured.** Neither arm has fully plateaued on the training loss: both are
still falling ~0.005–0.006 per epoch over the last five, with the cosine rate already at ~1e-5 to 0.
On the **held-out primary cell, however, both arms have plateaued**: arm3r's in-training probe is
flat at 0.49–0.50 from epoch 23 onward (16 epochs, 3 probe points), arm2long's moves 0.42 → 0.41
over the same span. The branch's own statistics plateau too (‖W_out‖ 142 from epoch 22, contribution
≈0.51 logits). So the comparison is **task-metric-converged at the primary cell but schedule-limited
on the training loss** — reported that way rather than claimed as convergence. Longer training would
lower the train loss further; the epoch-23-to-39 probe says it would not move the primary cell.
The in-training probe is a probe, not a result: it is a single-cell batched forward and not part of
the per-item artifacts.

## 8. What this does and does not establish

* **H5 is not resolved in the positive direction by this arm.** Adding a parallel cross-attention
  branch to the pre-trained head, init-fair and with three times the optimisation steps, does **not**
  produce a significant primary-cell gain over the schedule-matched fine-tuned head: +7.5 pp,
  p=0.086, Holm 1.0. The pre-registered conclusion for that outcome is *"the fix is adaptation, not
  design"*.
* **The mechanism is falsified independently of the accuracy numbers.** The zeroed-branch ablation
  is statistically indistinguishable from the arm at every cell (≤2.5 % of decisions change). A
  reader who ignores significance and reads the +7.5 pp as a win would be crediting the branch for
  something the branch does not do.
* **`arm1 < arm2 < arm3r` at the primary cell is not a ladder of mechanisms.** 0.365 → 0.455 → 0.485
  is: adaptation (+9.0 pp, p=0.0021), then a non-significant further +3.0 pp (p=0.512) that the
  ablation attributes to the training trajectory.
* **A negative that is not nothing.** The programme now knows that this class of intervention —
  bolting a fresh attention stage onto a pre-trained decision head with a zero-initialised output —
  is trainable, harmless at `L0`, and inference-inert, and it has the ablation to prove the last
  claim rather than assert it. The cross-attention *replacement* arm (arm3) remains a dead arm, not
  a fair test of the architecture (L-037).
* **Limits.** (i) Single seed per arm; the +7.5 pp is inside trajectory noise, and the two runs are
  not RNG-matched. (ii) The comparison is underpowered at n=200 to exclude an effect below ≈5 pp
  (M-001/L-022). (iii) `arm2long` confirms that 40 epochs is a *worse* primary cell than 12 for the
  plain head (0.410 vs 0.455) — with one seed, both numbers carry that caveat. (iv) Synthetic
  needle-in-haystack only (`protocol.md` §10): nothing here is a claim about real long documents.
  (v) Driver limitation: `run_arm3r.py` is stage-major, so both arms trained before either was
  evaluated, which delayed the primary-cell verdict by ≈60 min; no result was affected.
