## 2. Pipeline integrity

All scripts are committed under `experiments/V-002/`; raw outputs beside them.

### 2.1 Leakage — train/eval split disjoint by content, not by id

`env/venv/bin/python experiments/h5-adapter/leakage_check.py` re-run at HEAD: **all gating checks
PASS** (`experiments/V-002/leakage_rerun.txt`). I then re-derived the split **with my own hashing and
n-gram code** (`verify_content.py`), because a check that calls the generator's own helpers proves
less:

* plan.json is exactly what `plan.py` regenerates — **eval 1600 items identical, train 1008 items
  identical** (A1).
* 96 eval templates vs 96 train templates: **0 shared** by sha256 of the canonical text; every
  template's recorded `sha256` equals my own hash of its text (A2).
* **0 shared rendered needles** out of 800 unique eval renders × 1008 train renders, by hash *and*
  by text; no eval render is a substring of any train render (A3/A3b).
* slot-value pools disjoint (their c7; my A2 re-hash agrees).

**Longest shared n-gram.** Both readings are reported because they answer different questions:

| comparison | longest shared word n-gram | example | content or function words? |
|---|---|---|---|
| templates, slots neutralised (their c4) | **4** | `i would like to` | **function words only** |
| templates, raw text (my A4b) | **4** | `i would like to` | function words only |
| **rendered needles** (my A4, the strict one) | **4** | `please tell me the` | 3 of 4 function words; the 4th is the generic verb `tell`; **no domain content word** |

Longest shared character n-gram: 19 over rendered needles (`" until the process "`) and 22 over raw
templates (`" of {n} dollars appear"`) — a morphological span, not template reuse. The gate is a word
n-gram ≤ 4, and it passes on both readings.

**Defect (filed as [#13](https://github.com/Parswanadh/laya-lab/issues/13)).** The committed
`leakage_report.json` is stale: `plan.json` was regenerated at `ffbdd7c` *after* the report was
written at `58c3b20`, and the train items changed. Its `c6_max_rendered_token_jaccard = 0.2667` is
therefore not reproducible from the committed inputs (HEAD gives 0.2222). Worse, `c6` is not the
maximum it claims to be: the true maximum over all 400×400 rendered train×eval pairs is **0.3158**
(`The remittance advice for TR-1359 …` / `The annual renewal for invoice 358 …`), because
`max_jaccard()` scores only the 8 candidates with the most shared 4-grams. The gate is `< 0.60`, so
**no verdict changes** — the number is wrong, the conclusion is not.

### 2.2 Eval-set construction, measured from the tokens

`verify_docs.py` rebuilds **all 2200 eval documents** with the committed `docs.py`/`plan.py`/pools
and the fork tokenizer, then checks the rebuilt prompt itself. 0 problems
(`experiments/V-002/verify_docs.json`):

* `needle_kept` **2200/2200** — and true *by reconstruction*, not merely by the recorded flag: the
  needle block's token-id slice is inside the kept prefix of the prompt in every row.
* Position: `needle_token_start == round(position × pad)` for all 2200, and it equals the value in
  `cache/eval/index.json` for all 2200.
* **`pad=7000` is exactly 7000 filler tokens**: for all 400 items of the two L7000 cells,
  `len(state) − len(needle block) = 7000` and `n_before + n_after = 7000` with
  `n_before = round(p·7000)`, `n_after = 7000 − n_before`. Same check: `pad=4000` → 1600/1600 items
  exactly 4000; `pad=0` → 200/200 items 0 filler.
* **No decode/re-tokenise round trip in the prompt**: the prompt's state slice is *identical* to the
  constructed `state_ids` in 2200/2200 items (`build_sequence(..., state_ids=…)` consumes ids; the
  `tok.decode(...)` in `docs.py` is only for the recorded preview). Prompts are 7059–7074 tokens at
  `L=7000`, and `state_tokens_kept` (= 7013…7028) is below the 8146-token room, so **nothing is
  truncated anywhere** — `needle_kept = 1.00` is structural, not marginal.
* `needle_kept` is 200/200 in **every cell of every arm's JSONL**, matching the cache.

## 3. The arm2 claim (ledger L-038)

Recomputed from `predictions/*.jsonl` with my own McNemar, bootstrap, Holm and oracle
implementations (`recompute_stats.py` → `verify_stats.json`).

**Primary numbers.**

| `L4000-p000` | 0.580 | 0.590 | +1.00 pp | 7 / 5 | 0.7744 | 1.0 ✗ |
| `L7000-p000` | 0.595 | 0.585 | −1.00 pp | 6 / 8 | 0.7905 | 1.0 ✗ |

**Test matches the design.** `stats.py` pairs by `item_id` (`M.pair_rows`) and calls
`M.mcnemar_exact` — correct for a paired design. It matters: the same data under an *independent*
two-proportion z-test gives p = 0.067 at `L7000-p100` and p = 0.108 at `L4000-p100`, i.e. **the
wrong test would have erased both results** (23–43× inflation). At `L4000-p050` the independent test
still gives 8.2e-04, so that cell is robust to the choice; `L7000-p100` is not.

**Pairing is real, verified by content.** 2200/2200 shared `item_id`s across all three files, with
**0** differences in `label`, `template_id`, `pad`, `needle_position`, `slot_gold`, `option_order`;
**0** differences in `input_tokens`, `needle_token_start`, `state_tokens_kept`, `truncated`,
`needle_kept`; no duplicate ids; every row's gold label equals the plan's label for that item. The
documents themselves are identified by `cache/eval/index.json`'s `state_sha256`, and my independent
rebuild reproduces that hash for all 2200 items (§2.2) — so "same `item_id`" demonstrably means the
same token-id document, not merely the same string.

**Bootstrap CIs** (percentile over items, 10,000 resamples, seed 12345 — the ledger's quoted
`[0.385, 0.525]` for arm2 at `L7000-p100` is reproduced exactly). But note for the promotion rule:
at both headline cells the arm1 and arm2 CIs **overlap** (`L7000-p100`: [0.300, 0.435] vs
[0.385, 0.525]; `L4000-p100`: [0.345, 0.485] vs [0.425, 0.565]). Only `L4000-p050` ([0.270, 0.400]
vs [0.430, 0.570]) and `L4000-p075` pass both the CI and the paired-test prongs. Filed as #15.

**Effect size beyond accuracy** (protocol §5 requires macro-F1 when the set is not perfectly
balanced in *predictions*, and it is not): `L7000-p100` 0.294 → **0.407**; `L4000-p050` 0.246 →
**0.440**; `L0` 0.573 → 0.559.

## 4. Mandatory attacks

### 4.1 Prior / position exploitation

* **Label–position correlation: none.** Every scored cell is exactly 50/50/50/50, so the
  position-only oracle is 0.2500, and 0.2500 is also the best single constant over the whole set.
* **A surface shortcut that needs no reading: none.** The slot values (`n=…`, `ref=…`) are shared
  across classes: leave-one-out accuracy from the slot key alone is **0.21–0.32** (chance = 0.25) in
  every cell. In-sample it reaches 0.79–0.84 purely by memorising 150-odd unique keys out of 200
  items — which is why the LOO number is the one that counts.
* **What a model *could* exploit is the content channel**, and that is the point: template id alone
  predicts the label at LOO 0.91–0.93, i.e. reading the request is worth ~0.92 minus 0.25 = 0.67 of
  accuracy.
* **Both arms are above the oracle in every scored cell, but arm1 barely**: 0.280/0.295/0.335 at the
  three mid-document cells, where the *per-label* breakdown shows why — arm1's accuracy on
  `gold=technical` is 0.96–1.00 there, i.e. its whole score is the "always technical" prior
  (50/200 = 0.250) plus ≤ 3.5 pp. Its accuracy on `gold=other` is **0.00** at every long distance
  (it never predicts `other` in most of those cells), and at `L4000-p050` its macro-F1 (0.246) is
  *below* the majority-class rate (0.250).
* **arm2's gain is a content gain with a prior cost.** Discordant pairs by gold label at
  `L7000-p100`: billing +17, sales +7, other +1, **technical −7**; at `L4000-p050`: billing +21,
  sales +15, other 0, **technical −3**. It recovers needles arm1 was collapsing over, and pays a few
  technical items for it. At `L0` the whole −2 pp is 4 `technical` items changing hands; billing and
  sales are identical (0.84/0.68 in both arms).
* The **swapped** control is the sharpest content test and it partly favours the claim, partly
  qualifies it: accuracy against the *original* label is exactly 0.250 for both arms, while
  accuracy against the *substituted* needle's class is **0.275** (arm1, chance) → **0.415** (arm2).
  So arm2 demonstrably follows content that arm1 does not — but 0.415 is far from 1.0, i.e. the
  fine-tuned head reads the needle **partially**, not reliably.

## 5. Seed attack

The committed artifacts contain arm2 at **seed 0 only** (`summary.json`: `seeds: [0]`; the only
`runs/arm2_shipped_init/seed0/` checkpoint exists). As committed, **the claim is a single-seed
result and is not a seed-spread result** — there is no seed spread to compare the gain against, so
protocol §6's "a gain smaller than the seed spread is not a gain" is **not satisfied by evidence**.

I attempted the attack: `run_seed_attack.sh` retrains arm2 at seeds 1 and 2 with the recipe recorded
in the seed-0 artifact (`epochs=12, lr=5e-4, wd=0.01, token_budget=12288, max_batch=8,
train_mode=uniform`; ≈18 min train + ≈2 min eval each) from the pinned revision and a frozen
snapshot of the fork tree. The GPU was held by the H5b agent's `stage_arm3r.sh` job throughout, and
my non-blocking lock attempts were refused. Retry log: `.scratch/V-002/seed_attack_retry.log`;
status at the time of writing: **NOT RUN — GPU lock refused** (see the closing note if this changed).

## 7. What this verification does **not** cover

* **Synthetic needle-in-haystack only** — nothing here transfers to real long documents
  (protocol §10).
* **English only** (`lang: "en"` in every plan item); no language sweep.
* **`arm3_xattn` / `arm4_xattn_long` mechanics** are out of my scope (issue #12 asks about arm1 and
  arm2); I only report their per-cell numbers where a control needed a third column.
* **The arm2 head checkpoints are not committed** (gitignored, 59 MB each). My re-run used the
  on-disk `runs/arm2_shipped_init/seed0/head.pt`; if that file were lost, arm2's eval would have to
  be regenerated by retraining, which I did **not** verify reproduces seed 0 bit-for-bit (my seed
  attack, had it run, would have been the first evidence either way).
* **Lock hygiene of the original runs is only partly evidenced.** `launch.log` shows the locked
  pipeline acquiring the lock at 00:09:51 and being terminated (exit 143) at 00:54:53; the arm2
  (00:58) and arm3 (01:06) prediction files were written *after* that, into ad-hoc `_eval_*.out`
  logs, with no lock record. Accuracy is not timing-sensitive and those rows carry `latency_s = 0`,
  so no reported number is affected — but the "timings taken with the GPU lock held" clause is not
  evidenced for those two runs. My own re-runs of both arms held the lock.
