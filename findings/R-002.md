# R-002 — Sliding-window widening and global/local layer reallocation: prior art

Owner: research (sub-agent A). Companion: R-001.md (RoPE scaling, hybrid architectures, benchmarks).

## Scope

This note covers two questions only:

- **Area A** — for a transformer **already trained** with a local/sliding attention window of size `W`, what is known about **raising `W` at inference** (post-hoc, no retraining)? Does it help, hurt, or is training required?
- **Area B** — can the **assignment of layers (or heads) to global vs. local attention be changed after training**, either by swapping the pattern in a trained checkpoint or by **adding** global-attention capacity at inference?

Out of scope: RoPE base/frequency scaling, context-extension fine-tuning recipes, KV-cache eviction policy design, and benchmark comparisons (R-001).

## A. Sliding-window widening at inference

### A1. Gemma 2 is the only paper I found that explicitly measures changing the sliding window at inference on an already-trained model — and it only tests *narrowing*
- **Source**: https://arxiv.org/html/2408.00118v3 (Table 10); abs: https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00118
- **What was measured**: Gemma 2 **9B**, perplexity on a validation set, with the local-attention sliding window changed at inference:
  | sliding window | 4096 (trained) | 2048 | 1024 |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | val. perplexity | **1.63** | **1.63** | **1.64** |
- **Authors' words**: "we show that we can change the sliding window size of the local attention layers of the models during inference with moderate impact on perplexity. Adjusting the size of the sliding window can thus be a leverage for slight inference speed gain."
- **Critical bound**: the tested direction is **4096 → 2048 → 1024, i.e. narrowing only**. **Widening above the trained 4096 is never tested.** The framing is explicitly a *speed* lever, not a quality lever.
- **Architecture context (why it is probably insensitive)**: Gemma 2 alternates local and global attention "in every other layer"; "The sliding window size of local attention layers is set to 4096 tokens, while the span of the global attention layers is set to 8192 tokens." With a global layer every other layer, the local window is not the information bottleneck.
- **By whom**: Gemma 2 authors.
- **Status**: paper-asserted.
- **Implies for us**: "window change at inference is cheap" has exactly one direct measurement in the literature I could find, it is one model, one metric (val perplexity, not a task), and it does **not** cover widening. A design that assumes widening is safe is relying on an untested extrapolation of a *narrowing* result.

### A2. ModernBERT's paper contains **no** inference-time window study, and its only attention-pattern ablation is reported without numbers
- **Source**: https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13663v2 (§3.1 "Alternating Attention"; Appendix D "Architecture ablations"; Table 4) — abs https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13663
- **Design (Table 4, both base and large)**: `Global Attention = Every three layers`; `Local Attention Window = 128`; `RoPE theta = 160,000`; `Local Attn RoPE theta = 10,000`.
- **Ablation (Appendix D, verbatim, no table, no numbers)**: "We explored the use of alternating global/local attention, with global attention every 3 layers and local attention over a 128 token sliding window otherwise. This setup yielded **identical downstream performance when compared to the use of global attention in every layer, even at 100 billion tokens**, while resulting in major speedups."
  - Note what this compares: **alternating (every 3rd global)** vs **all-global**. There is **no local-only arm**, no window-size sweep, and no per-benchmark table.
- **What the paper says about `local_attention` at inference**: nothing. A case-insensitive search of the full text for "at inference", "inference time", "inference-time" returned **zero** matches.
- **Long-context caveat the authors themselves flag (speculation, not measurement)**: on single-vector long-context retrieval (MLDR/DPR), ModernBERT "performs noticeably worse than GTE-en-MLM", and they write: "We plan to explore multiple potential explanations for this phenomenon in future work, **including the impact of local attention**". They also *theorize* the opposite in the ColBERT multi-vector setting, where they "leave further exploration of this phenomenon to future work."
- **Upstream config confirms the knobs are plain config fields**: `answerdotai/ModernBERT-base/config.json` → `"local_attention": 128`, `"global_attn_every_n_layers": 3`, `"local_rope_theta": 10000.0`, `"global_rope_theta": 160000.0`, `"max_position_embeddings": 8192`. Source: https://huggingface.co/answerdotai/ModernBERT-base/raw/main/config.json
- **Upstream semantics gotcha (retrieved from source)**: in `transformers`, `ModernBertConfig.sliding_window` is a *property* over `local_attention`, **not** the window itself — `sliding_window` returns `local_attention // 2` and the setter writes `local_attention = value * 2`. Source: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/huggingface/transformers/main/src/transformers/models/modernbert/configuration_modernbert.py (lines ~160–167; docstring: "Half-window size: `local_attention` is the total window, so we divide by 2."). The config docstring also warns: "In local attention layers, the CLS token doesn't attend to all tokens on long sequences."
- **Status**: paper-asserted (paper); upstream-reported, retrieved verbatim (config/source).
- **Implies for us**: the specific claim "ModernBERT's `local_attention` can be raised at inference" is **unsupported by the ModernBERT paper**. The paper's ablation is about *interleaving ratio at training time*, and its own long-context retrieval gap is left as an open question that the authors explicitly attribute (tentatively) to local attention.

### A3. Longformer's window-size ablation is a **training-time** ablation, and Longformer's own recipe widens the window only *while continuing to train*
- **Source**: https://arxiv.org/html/2004.05150v2 (§4.2.2 + Table 4; §4.1; §5 "Attention Pattern"; Table 12) — abs https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.05150
- **Table 4 numbers** (small model, `text8`, character-level LM, **150K steps of the phase-1 config**, dev BPC, lower is better):
  | configuration | dev BPC |
  |---|---|
  | Decreasing `w` (512 → 32) | 1.24 |
  | Fixed `w` (= 230) | 1.23 |
  | Increasing `w` (32 → 512) | **1.21** |
  | No dilation | 1.21 |
  | Dilation on 2 heads | 1.20 |
  - Authors' caveat on this table: "One caveat is that the ordering of end performance will not agree with that at step 150K."
  - Each arm is **trained** with its window configuration. Nothing here is post-hoc.
- **Longformer widens windows only with continued training**: staged training "where we increase the attention window size and sequence length across multiple training phases… on each subsequent phase, we double the window size and the sequence length, and halve the learning rate." Table 12: Phase 1 window sizes 32 (bottom) – 8,192 (top); Phase 5 window sizes 512 (bottom) – [top value blank in the retrieved HTML].
- **Receptive-field statements (relevant to Area B)**: "In a transformer with ℓ layers, the receptive field size at the top layer is **ℓ × w** (assuming `w` is fixed for all layers)." With dilation `d`: "the receptive field is **ℓ × d × w**." On global attention: "we make this attention operation **symmetric**: a token with a global attention attends to all tokens across the sequence, and all tokens in the sequence attend to it."
- **A negative result about changing the pattern post-hoc**: footnote 6 — "Adding dilation on a few heads as in §4.1 **hurt** performance, likely because it is not compatible with the pretrained RoBERTa weights. **Retraining such model from scratch might be needed** to improve it." (The dilated variant scores *better* 1.20 when trained from the phase-1 config, Table 4 — the failure is specifically when bolted onto RoBERTa's pretrained weights.)
- **Plug-in claim**: "Note that our attention pattern can be plugged into any pretrained transformer model without the need to change the model architecture." In practice this was done with **continued pretraining from RoBERTa**, not at inference.
- **Status**: paper-asserted.
- **Implies for us**: the closest classical-encoder precedent for changing a window is *continue training with a window schedule*. The one documented post-hoc pattern change (dilation on RoBERTa weights) **hurt and was attributed to weight/pattern incompatibility**.

### A4. BigBird: **no window-size ablation found**, in either direction
- **Source**: https://arxiv.org/html/2007.14062v2 (searched for "window size", "block size", "ablation") — abs https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14062
- **What is there**: definitions (`w = 3×b`, `r = 3×b`, `g = 2×b`, `b = 64` for GLUE), block-sparse kernel construction details, and a genomics-side "simple ablation study to validate the hypothesis that, similar to NLP, having a larger context improves performance" (context length, not window size).
- **Status**: negative search result (nothing to cite).
- **Implies for us**: BigBird cannot be cited either for or against window-size sensitivity.

### A5. Mistral 7B: the trained window is `W = 4096`; the *vendor* later shipped checkpoints with SWA disabled — but the change is confounded and is a new checkpoint, not a post-hoc change
- **Paper**: https://arxiv.org/html/2310.06825v1 — abs https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06825
  - "each token can attend to at most W tokens from the previous layer"; Table 1 `window_size = 4096`.
  - "At the last layer, using a window size of W = 4096, we have a theoretical attention span of approximately **131K tokens**." "after k attention layers, information can move forward by up to **k × W** tokens."
  - "**Note that tokens outside the sliding window still influence next word prediction.**"
  - Rolling buffer: "A fixed attention span means that we can limit our cache size using a rolling buffer cache"; for chunked prefill "we can select the window size as our chunk size."
  - Efficiency claim: for 16K sequence length and W=4096, "changes made to FlashAttention and xFormers yield a **2x** speed improvement over a vanilla attention baseline" (paper-asserted, hardware/version unspecified in the text I read).
- **Upstream config facts I retrieved** (raw `config.json`):
  | checkpoint | `sliding_window` | `rope_theta` | `max_position_embeddings` |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `mistralai/Mistral-7B-v0.1` | **4096** | 10000.0 | 32768 |
  | `mistralai/Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.1` | **4096** | 10000.0 | 32768 |
  | `mistralai/Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2` | **null** | 1000000.0 | 32768 |
  | `mistralai/Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3` | **null** | 1000000.0 | 32768 |
  - URLs: `https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Mistral-7B-v0.1/raw/main/config.json`, `…/Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2/raw/main/config.json`, etc.
  - v0.2 model card bullets (retrieved): "32k context window (vs 8k context in v0.1)", "Rope-theta = 1e6", "**No Sliding-Window Attention**". https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2/raw/main/README.md
- **Honest reading**: this is **not** evidence that widening works post-hoc. v0.2 is a *different checkpoint*, and it changed `rope_theta` (1e4 → 1e6) at the same time. It is evidence only that a vendor was willing to ship SWA-off inference configs, and that the SWA↔no-SWA switch was bundled with a retrain + RoPE change.
- **Status**: paper-asserted (Mistral paper); upstream-reported, retrieved verbatim (configs, model card).
- **Implies for us**: do not cite Mistral as a post-hoc widening datapoint; the confound (new weights + RoPE base change) makes it unusable for that purpose.

### A6. StreamingLLM: **widening the inference window on a dense-trained model frequently makes perplexity worse** — this is the cleanest negative result I found
- **Source**: https://arxiv.org/html/2309.17453v3 (§4.4 "Cache Sizes", Table 6; §3.1, Table 1) — abs https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17453
- **Table 6 — "Effects of cache size on StreamingLLM's performance"**. Cache config `x+y` = `x` initial (sink) tokens + `y` recent tokens; perplexity on 400K tokens of concatenated PG19; **the model and all weights are fixed within each row — only the attended window changes**:
  | model | 4+252 | 4+508 | 4+1020 | 4+2044 | 4+4092 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Falcon-7B | 13.61 | 12.84 | **12.34** | 12.84 ↑worse | — |
  | MPT-7B | **14.12** | 14.25 | 14.33 | 14.99 ↑worse | — |
  | Pythia-12B | 13.17 | 12.52 | **12.08** | 12.09 | — |
  | Llama-2-7B | — | 9.73 | 9.32 | **9.08** | 9.59 ↑worse |
  - Authors' conclusion, verbatim: "**Contrary to intuition, increasing the cache size doesn't consistently lower the language modeling perplexity.** This inconsistency shows a potential limitation where these models might not maximize the utility of the entire context they receive."
  - Direction of the result: **widening the attended window (1020→2044, or 2044→4092) made PPL worse on 3 of the 4 model rows**; MPT-7B is monotonically worse from 252 upward.
- **Table 1 — window-only attention is catastrophic, but the sink ablation is size-matched**: Llama-2-13B, first 65K tokens of PG19: `0+1024 (Window)` PPL **5158.07**; `4+1020` PPL **5.40**; `4"\n"+1020` PPL **5.60**. (Same total cache size — this isolates the *sink*, not the window.)
- **Table 2 — saturation in added prefix tokens**: Llama-2-7B on `0+4096 / 1+4095 / 2+4094 / 4+4092 / 8+4088` → **3359.95 / 11.88 / 10.51 / 9.59 / 9.54**. (Different total sizes; the "0+y" arm is the window-only arm.)
- **The paper explicitly declines the widening question**: "**We do not expand the attention window size of LLMs** or enhance the model's memory and usage on long texts."
- **Caveat on transfer**: these are **dense-trained** models run with a rolling window at inference — the *reverse* mismatch from our question. Results may not transfer to a window-trained model.
- **Status**: paper-asserted.
- **Implies for us**: the naive prior "a bigger window is weakly better" is **measured false** in a controlled same-weights setting on 3 of 4 models, including a monotone regression on MPT-7B. Widening must be treated as a change that can degrade, not as a free monotone improvement.

- **Source**: https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10411v2 ("SWAA: Sliding Window Attention Adaptation for Efficient and Quality Preserving Long Context Processing") — abs https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.10411

### A8. Training-free sparsity at inference on dense-trained models degrades 7B-scale models badly; scale-dependent and task-dependent
- **Source**: https://arxiv.org/html/2504.17768v1 ("The Sparse Frontier: Sparse Attention Trade-offs in Transformer LLMs") — abs https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.17768
- **What was measured**: six training-free sparse patterns, models 7B–72B, sequences 16K–128K, sparsity 0–95%, aggregated over 9 tasks (RULER NIAH/VT/CWE/SQuAD + QuALITY, TOEFL + three new naturalistic tasks).
- **Key numbers**: Qwen 7B, decoding with the Quest pattern, dense → 20× compression: **0.4 → 0.24 at 32k (40% drop)** and **0.29 → 0.06 at 128k (79% drop)**. 32B/72B: "gaps consistently below 0.05 even at 20× compression". Prefilling: "performance gaps between dense and highly sparse models remain relatively uniform (**0.1–0.15**) across all model sizes".
- **Headline caution**: "Even moderate sparsity levels often result in significant performance degradation on at least one task, highlighting that sparse attention is not a universal solution"; "almost every configuration has at least one task where maximum tolerable compression is below 5×" (72B Quest being the only exception).
- **Caveat**: sparsification is *narrowing* attention, not widening. It is evidence about **post-hoc attention-pattern change on fixed weights**, which is the general class our question belongs to.
- **Status**: paper-asserted.
- **Implies for us**: at ~7B scale, post-hoc attention-pattern changes have been measured to be fragile and task-dependent; aggregate averages hide per-task collapse. Any claim of the form "we changed the attention pattern at inference and it was fine" needs per-task reporting.

### A9. Positional encoding × window size: **no source found that measures this interaction.** Partial facts that do exist:
- **RoPE is a relative encoding**: "the proposed RoPE encodes the absolute position with a rotation matrix and meanwhile incorporates the explicit relative position dependency in self-attention formulation", with "decaying inter-token dependency with increasing relative distances". Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.09864 (RoFormer). *paper-asserted.*
- **ModernBERT deliberately uses two different RoPE bases inside one checkpoint**: `local_rope_theta = 10,000` for the 128-token local layers, `global_rope_theta = 160,000` for the every-3rd global layers (Table 4 and `config.json`, URLs in A2). *paper-asserted / upstream-reported.* This means a widened local layer and its neighbouring global layer would be using different frequency bases — a fact, not a measured consequence.
- **ModernBERT's long-context extension changed only the global layers' theta**: "we extend the native context length of ModernBERT to 8192 tokens by increasing the **global attention layer's** RoPE theta to 160,000 and train for an additional 300 billion tokens." *paper-asserted.* The local layers' theta was left at 10,000.
- **Length generalization of explicit PEs is poor in a decoder-only setting**: "the most commonly used positional encoding methods, such as ALiBi, Rotary, and APE, are not well suited for length generalization in downstream tasks"; "NoPE outperforms other explicit positional encoding methods while requiring no additional computation." Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19466v1 (`v3` and `v4` 404; `v1` and `v2` resolve) — "The Impact of Positional Encoding on Length Generalization in Transformers". *paper-asserted, decoder-only models trained from scratch on reasoning tasks — not a windowed encoder.*
- **ALiBi's framing**: "how does a model achieve extrapolation at inference time for sequences that are longer than it saw during training? We first show that extrapolation can be enabled by simply…" (linear biases). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.12409. *paper-asserted.*
- **UNRETRIEVED**: I found **no** study that measures whether widening a locally-trained layer's window (so it sees relative distances it never saw) breaks calibration, and **no** study of RoPE-base mismatch between a widened layer and its neighbours. See the UNRETRIEVED section.

### A10. Does the receptive field of an alternating local/global stack make the local window irrelevant? Sources disagree in emphasis
- **`layers × window ≥ context` is explicitly called inadequate**: RATTENTION — "simply ensuring that `number_of_layers × window_size ≥ context_length` is often an **inadequate heuristic** for determining the optimal window size." Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15545v2 (abs https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.15545). *paper-asserted.*
- **Mistral's counter-framing**: information moves forward `k × W` per `k` layers, giving a "theoretical attention span of approximately 131K tokens" at W=4096/32 layers — a *reachability* statement, not a quality claim. (A5, paper-asserted.)
- **Longformer's counter-framing**: receptive field is `ℓ × w` (or `ℓ × d × w` with dilation), "where top layers have access to all input locations". (A3, paper-asserted.)
- **RATTENTION's measured window/quality curve (training-time ablation)**: 3B local-global models trained on 400B tokens, MMLU 5-shot vs window size, with a full-attention baseline: "there is a clear tradeoff between window size and MMLU performance—smaller window sizes lead to reduced performance"; RAttention with window **≥ 512** matches or surpasses the full-attention baseline. At 2T tokens / 3B, "RAttention with a window size of 512 outperforms SWA models using window sizes up to 2048"; the same holds at 12B / 600B tokens. *paper-asserted. All training-time.*
- **Gemma 3's version, on trained models**: local:global ratio ablation (Fig. 3) — "We observe **minimal impact on perplexity** when changing this ratio"; "The impact is minimal, even with **7-to-1** local to global." Sliding-window sweep (Fig. 4) — "The sliding window can be **reduced significantly** without impacting perplexity" (2B models, text-only, 1:1 and 1:3 local:global). Memory at 32k prefill: "global only" = **60%** overhead; "1:3, sw=1024" = **<15%**. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2503.19786v1 (abs https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.19786). *paper-asserted; reduction direction only; figures, not tables — I did not extract numeric curve values.*
- **Implies for us**: the literature supports "with enough global/interleaved layers, local window size has weak leverage on quality" (Gemma 2/3, ModernBERT) and simultaneously warns that `layers × window` arithmetic is not a sufficient condition (RATTENTION). Both are training-time statements. Neither tells you what happens if you *raise* `W` post-hoc.

## B. Global/local layer reallocation post-hoc

### B1. ModernBERT's own interleaving ablation compares *alternating* vs *all-global* — it does not test local-only, and reports no numbers
- Already detailed in **A2**. The single relevant sentence (Appendix D) claims "identical downstream performance … even at 100 billion tokens" for global-every-3-layers vs global-every-layer. No table, no per-task numbers, no window sweep, no "what if we change the pattern after training".
- The knobs are exposed as config (`global_attn_every_n_layers`, `local_attention`), so a post-hoc swap is *mechanically* possible; **nothing in the paper or the repo measures it.**
- **Status**: paper-asserted (design + unquantified ablation); upstream-reported (config fields).

### B2. Gemma's local:global ratio and window sweeps are **training-time** ablations, and they say the pattern has little leverage
- **Gemma 3** (§5.2, Fig. 3–6): "In Fig. 3, we compare different ratios of local to global attention layers. 1:1 is used in Gemma 2 models, and 5:1 is used in Gemma 3. We observe **minimal impact on perplexity** when changing this ratio." · "The impact is minimal, even with **7-to-1** local to global." · "The sliding window can be **reduced significantly** without impacting perplexity." · KV/activation memory with a 32k pre-filled cache: "**global only** … a memory overhead of **60%**, while this is reduced to **less than 15%** with 1:3 and sliding windows of 1024". Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2503.19786v1. *paper-asserted. Read off figure captions; I did not extract numeric curve values from the figures.* Also stated: these ablations "are run with text-only models", i.e. 2B scale.
- **Gemma 2** (§"Local Sliding Window and Global Attention"): alternate local/global "in every other layer"; local = 4096, global span = 8192. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2408.00118v3. *paper-asserted.*
- **Status**: paper-asserted. **No independent reproduction found.**
- **Implies for us**: the claim "the local:global ratio barely matters" rests on **vendor-internal 2B-scale perplexity figures with no numbers in the text**. It is directionally consistent across two model generations (Gemma 2, Gemma 3) and with ModernBERT's unquantified ablation — three independent-ish vendor reports agreeing that the ratio is low-leverage, none of them publishing a table.

### B3. SWAA's "Interleaving Layers" is the only *post-hoc* layer-pattern reallocation I found with numbers — and alone it recovers little
- Source and full table: **A7** (https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10411v2, Table 1).
- **Post-hoc layer swap, everything else fixed** (Qwen3-4B, 2k window, no sinks, no FA decode): all-SWA **3.2 / 11.0** (think/non-think) → FA layers `[1,3,5,…]` **13.4 / 18.4** → FA layers `[0,2,4,…]` (measured only with keep-10 + FA decode: 36.0 / 17.2). Full-attention baseline **73.0 / 62.0**.
- **Which layers**: they try `[1,3,5,…]`, `[0,2,4,…]`, `[1,5,9,…]`, `[1,9,17,…]`, `[3,7,11,…]` (rows 5, 10, 17, 19, 20, 21). Best is `[1,3,5,…]`; note Gemma-2 also uses `[1,3,5,…]` and Gemma-3 uses `[5,11,17,…]` (stated in their §3.2).
- Their own framing: "for an FA-pretrained model, **layers may exhibit distinct behaviors**, suggesting it may not be optimal to simply assign SWA to layers `[1,3,5,…]`. Instead, it might be preferable to use statistical features to select 'lazy' (mostly focusing on just recent tokens) layers, as adopted by **LightTransfer**… However, we find that **LightTransfer is not consistently superior in practice**."
- **Status**: paper-asserted. **No independent reproduction found.**
- **Implies for us**: post-hoc layer reallocation *does* move the needle, but the published numbers say it is a **partial** fix that must be combined with other mechanisms; and data-driven layer selection did **not** beat a fixed stride in their hands. That is a negative result about "smart" post-hoc layer selection.

### B4. DuoAttention: post-hoc, **head-level** (not layer-level) reallocation works near-losslessly — but only after an optimization pass, and only if the "retrieval heads" are found correctly
- **Source**: https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10819v1 ("DuoAttention: Efficient Long-Context LLM Inference with Retrieval and Streaming Heads") — abs https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10819
- **Mechanism**: each layer keeps **two** KV caches — full for *retrieval heads*, constant-size (sinks + recent tokens) for *streaming heads*. Classification comes from a **learned per-KV-head gate** α optimized on a synthetic passkey dataset; "this training phase is efficient, **requiring only the gate values to be trainable—leaving all other model parameters frozen**—thus allowing it to be completed within several hours on an 8 GPU node." Gates are then **binarized** at a threshold τ. So: **post-hoc pattern change on frozen weights, but not training-free.**
- **Headline results (paper-asserted)**: "DuoAttention achieves performance comparable to full attention on most tasks, using a **25% KV cache budget for MHA** and a **50% KV cache budget for GQA**"; full LongBench results at those budgets (Tables 3–4); "with a 50% KV cache budget, DuoAttention achieves near-lossless performance on most benchmarks"; reported speedups "up to 2.55× for MHA and 1.67× for GQA models", decoding "up to 2.18×/1.50×", pre-filling "up to 1.73×/1.63×".
- **The load-bearing negative result**: the passkey figure shows "the model's performance drops significantly when the middle tokens in the KV cache of **retrieval heads** are pruned, i.e., replaced with streaming attention. In contrast, removing the middle tokens for **streaming heads** has no significant impact on passkey retrieval accuracy." I.e. **which** components you make local is the whole game; a uniform local-window policy is what fails.
- **Status**: paper-asserted. **No independent reproduction found.** (DuoAttention's own comparison against FastGen is a *reimplementation* by the authors of a third-party method — https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10819v1 §"FastGen" — which is author-run, not independent.)
- **Implies for us**: head-level is the granularity that the literature has actually made work; layer-level uniform strides are the granularity that has been measured to underperform. Any "make the local layers global again" plan is coarser than the best-known post-hoc method.

### B5. Inference-time depth augmentation: only *looping with regularization* is training-free, and naive layer repetition is catastrophic
- **"Training-Free Looped Transformers"** — https://arxiv.org/html/2605.23872v1 (abs https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23872). *paper-asserted, verified by me from the full text.*
  - Mechanism: "a lightweight inference-time wrapper **loops a contiguous mid-stack block of layers of a frozen checkpoint** without additional fine-tuning, continued training, or architectural changes."
  - Reported gains: Qwen3-4B-Instruct **MMLU-Pro 5-shot 57.14 → 59.79 (+2.64 pp)**; GPQA-Main 0-shot **33.71 → 35.71 (+2.01)**; CommonsenseQA 7-shot **78.87 → 79.93 (+1.06)**; Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct +1.14 CommonsenseQA; Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B-Chat **+2.30 ARC-Challenge**; Moonlight-16B-A3B +1.20 OpenBookQA; DeepSeek-V2-Lite-Chat ARC-C 57.94 → 58.79.
  - **Naive looping collapses**: "Qwen3-0.6B-Instruct … naive **K = 4** looping … resulted in **lambada perplexity blowing up from ~13 to 1054.12**, and 16-task macro dropped **−10.21 pp**." (Verified verbatim in Appendix G.)
  - **Window size matters and has a cliff** (Table 2a, Qwen3-1.7B-Base, forward Euler K=2, 16-task macro Δ): n=1 +0.18 · n=2 +0.06 · n=3 +0.21 · **n=4 +0.55** · n=6 **−0.82** · n=12 **−0.63** · n=28 (whole stack) **−27.73**. The winning recipe is a *damped* Euler sub-step (`h = 1/K`) or an RK/β-interpolation, not `x ← g(x)`.
  - **MoE caveat**: block-mode looping thrashes expert routing; layer-mode (pin the routing decision) is required, worth "+0.5 to +1.7 pp on hard MoE benchmarks" over block-mode.
- **"Inner Loop Inference for Pretrained Transformers: Unlocking Latent Capabilities Without Training"** — https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14759v1 (abs https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14759). *paper-asserted, verified by me from the full text.* "We apply the naive looping strategy to the Gemma2-2B model on the WinoGrande benchmark, using a loop count of **3** and sweeping over **all start-end layer pairs**. **All looped configurations perform worse than the baseline accuracy, with some settings collapsing to near-chance performance.**" With regularization (uniform / moving-average / auto-align) they report "modest but consistent accuracy gains"; "**Llama-3-8B shows a more variable response across tasks**, indicating greater sensitivity to repeated iteration in this pre-norm architecture."
- **"Retrofitting Recurrent Depth into a Pretrained Language Model…"** — https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11233 (title verified; body not read by me) — reported to require training a bridge module, i.e. **not** training-free. **Status: title URL verified; characterization reporter-asserted.**
- **Status of the whole B5 cluster**: paper-asserted for 2605.23872 and 2602.14759 (I read the relevant sections); **no independent reproduction found for any of them**.
- **Implies for us**: "add depth at inference" is *not* free — the training-free versions work only in a narrow regime (a small mid-stack window, small K, damped/regularized update), and the naive version of exactly what a "duplicate a global layer" plan would do is measured to blow up perplexity by ~2 orders of magnitude.

## Negative results (prominent)

Ordered by how much they should constrain a design decision.

1. **Widening a sliding window at inference, with weights fixed, has been measured to make perplexity *worse*.** StreamingLLM Table 6, 400K tokens of PG19 (https://arxiv.org/html/2309.17453v3): MPT-7B PPL **14.12 → 14.25 → 14.33 → 14.99** as the attended window grows 252 → 508 → 1020 → 2044 (monotone, strictly worse); Falcon-7B **12.34 → 12.84** from 1020 → 2044; Llama-2-7B **9.08 → 9.59** from 2044 → 4092. Authors: "increasing the cache size doesn't consistently lower the language modeling perplexity." *paper-asserted.*
2. **The only paper that changes a trained model's sliding window at inference tests only the *narrowing* direction.** Gemma 2 9B: 4096/2048/1024 → val PPL **1.63 / 1.63 / 1.64** (https://arxiv.org/html/2408.00118v3, Table 10). Widening is untested. *paper-asserted.*
3. **Post-hoc layer reallocation alone recovers only a fraction of full-attention quality.** Qwen3-4B, LongMemEval_24k, all-SWA 2k window **3.2 / 11.0**, + interleaved FA layers `[1,3,5,…]` **13.4 / 18.4**, vs full attention **73.0 / 62.0** (https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10411v2, Table 1). Authors: "Naive SWA is not viable"; window size "is not the decisive role"; interleaving + FA decode are "the dominant drivers". *paper-asserted.*
4. **Naive inference-time depth repetition blows up.** Qwen3-0.6B-Instruct, naive K=4 block looping: LAMBADA perplexity **~13 → 1054.12**, 16-task macro **−10.21 pp**; and the *whole-stack* loop window (n=28) gives **−27.73 pp** while n=4 gives **+0.55 pp** (https://arxiv.org/html/2605.23872v1). Separately, **every** start–end loop pair on Gemma2-2B/WinoGrande was worse than baseline, some near-chance (https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14759v1). *paper-asserted.*
5. **Changing an attention pattern on a pretrained checkpoint was attributed to weight incompatibility and required retraining.** Longformer footnote 6: adding dilation to a few heads on RoBERTa weights "hurt performance, likely because it is not compatible with the pretrained RoBERTa weights. Retraining such model from scratch might be needed" (https://arxiv.org/html/2004.05150v2). *paper-asserted.*
6. **Post-hoc sparsification of attention is scale- and task-fragile.** Qwen 7B decoding, dense → 20× compression: **0.4 → 0.24** (32k) and **0.29 → 0.06** (128k); 32B/72B stay within 0.05; "almost every configuration has at least one task where maximum tolerable compression is below 5×" (https://arxiv.org/html/2504.17768v1). *paper-asserted.*
7. **Data-driven selection of which layers go local did not beat a fixed stride.** SWAA §3.2: LightTransfer's "lazy layer" selection "is **not consistently superior in practice**". *paper-asserted.*
8. **`--swa-full` (the one shipped "widen the SWA window to full" switch in llama.cpp) is documented by its author as a cache-correctness fallback, and the issues about it are all performance/memory bugs, not quality wins** — #24628 (50-55 → 26 T/s), #23978 (VRAM expands to f16-equivalent under KV quant), #25295 (save/load state lossy), #26122 (crash with `-sm tensor`). *Upstream-reported; titles API-verified, bodies reporter-asserted.*
9. **An inference-time window change can happen by accident through config serialization.** transformers #47940: Gemma `sliding_window` **512 → 257 → 129 → 65 → … → 2**, "2 is a fixed point, so the window keeps shrinking until attention is effectively local to one token" (https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/pull/47940). *Upstream-reported, retrieved verbatim.*
10. **Zero independently-reproduced results were found in either area.** Every measured number in this document is paper-asserted (or upstream-reported). I found no third-party replication of Gemma 2's Table 10, SWAA, StreamingLLM's cache-size table, DuoAttention, or either looping paper. That is itself a finding: the constraint set for this design decision currently rests entirely on author-run measurements.
