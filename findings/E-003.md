## 1. The question, and the control that makes it answerable

`findings/P1b-information-location.md` measured that the frozen encoder delivers
label-recoverable information to the option-marker positions at **0.820** (linear probe,
held-out templates, needle 4000 tokens away) while the shipped head returns **0.300** on the same
items. The headroom is therefore in the **aggregation path**, not the encoder.

A probe fitted on the evaluation distribution is not a fair like-for-like comparison against a
frozen head trained on a different task. So a win for a new head could be **training**, not
**architecture**. Arm 2 is the control that decides this, and it is the point of the experiment.

| arm | aggregation | initialisation | training data |
|---|---|---|---|
| `arm1_frozen` | shipped self-attention | shipped checkpoint | none — the baseline |
| `arm2_shipped_init` | shipped self-attention | **shipped checkpoint head**, then trained | uniform grid |
| `arm2_random_init` | shipped self-attention | random | uniform grid |
| `arm3_xattn` | **cross-attention** (4 heads) over all state tokens | random | uniform grid |
| `arm4_xattn_long` | cross-attention | random | long-distance oversampled |

**If arm 2 ≈ arm 3, the architecture is not the win**, and the honest conclusion is that the fix is
adaptation, not design. That outcome is pre-registered in P1b and is a perfectly good result.

Arm 3 follows the issue's specification: `Q = W_q H_M`, `K = W_v H_D` over **every** encoded state
token, `H_M <- H_M + MHA(Q,K,V) + FFN(.)`, ~2 layers, 4 heads, then the unchanged marker gather and
`scorer`. Only state tokens are keys/values; the decision block is masked on the key side, which is
what makes it cross-attention over the document rather than a second round of self-attention over
the prompt (`tests/test_cross_attention_head.py` asserts it).

### Parameter matching — computed, not asserted

`arms.analytic_parameter_table()`, **measured** (module shapes, no checkpoint needed):

| | shipped self-attention, 12 heads | cross-attention, 4 heads |
|---|---|---|
| aggregation stage, 2 layers | 14,175,744 | 14,175,744 |
| `type_emb` + `scorer` | 595,201 | 595,201 |
| **trainable total** | **14,770,945** | **14,770,945** |

Equal by construction: `nn.MultiheadAttention` costs `4·d²` whether or not query and key/value are
the same tensor, and head count sets per-head *width* rather than parameter count. The encoder
(≈307 M), the `act_head` and the temperature buffers are frozen in **every** arm, including the
baseline. `tests/test_cross_attention_head.py` re-derives the equality at `d=768` and on a tiny
model, and `train.py` raises if the encoder receives a gradient.

## 2. Data

Pools: `experiments/h5-adapter/pools/`, authored by `author_pools.py`.

| | train | eval |
|---|---|---|
| templates | 96 (24 per class × 4 classes) | 96 (24 per class × 4 classes) |
| of which | all newly authored | 12/class are the **verbatim P1b probe templates** (evaluation-only), 12/class new |
| slots | `{n}` × 96 values, `{ref}` × 96 values | disjoint value pools |

**Training distribution, stated explicitly** (`plan.json: train.regime_weights`) — a stratified grid
over 21 `(length, position)` regimes × 48 items, 12 per class:

```
pad ∈ {0, 1000, 2000, 4000, 7000}  ×  position ∈ {0.00, 0.25, 0.50, 0.75, 1.00}
pad = 0 has no position axis  →  4 × 5 + 1 = 21 regimes  →  1008 training items
```
Every regime contributes 48 items and every regime is label-balanced, so no length and no position
dominates. Within a class the surface forms are dealt from one shuffled combination pool, so every
training item is a **distinct** needle.

Arm 4's distribution (`plan.json: train_long_boost.regime_weights`) is the **same items**, with the
four long-distance regimes `(4000, 0.75) (4000, 1.00) (7000, 0.75) (7000, 1.00)` oversampled ×4 by
adding copies in their own cells. Reusing identical items is what keeps arm 4 a statement about the
distribution rather than about different data.

**Evaluation cells** — `n = 200` each, 50 per class, label-balanced by construction:

| cell | pad | needle position | note |
|---|---|---|---|
| `L0` | 0 | — | the short-context regression cell (`protocol.md` §2) |
| `L4000-p000 … p100` | 4000 | 0.00 / 0.25 / 0.50 / 0.75 / 1.00 | position sweep at **fixed** `L` |
| `L7000-p000`, `L7000-p100` | 7000 | 0.00 / 1.00 | second length anchor |
| `L4000-p100-perm` | 4000 | 1.00 | same items, **options reordered** |
| `L4000-p100-ablated` | 4000 | 1.00 | same haystacks, **needle replaced** by a sentence with no class cue |
| `L4000-p100-swapped` | 4000 | 1.00 | same haystacks, **needle replaced by another class's** needle |

**The position sweep is content-controlled.** The five `L4000-p*` cells reuse the *same* 200
needles, and the filler is seeded on `(needle, pad)` — **not** on the position — and the needle is
inserted into the filler's **token ids** at `round(position · pad)`. So the five documents differ
only in where the evidence sits inside byte-identical content. That is what makes a distance curve
a statement about position rather than about five different documents.
`check_docs.py` verifies it: needle offsets `[0, 1000, 2000, 3000, 4000]`, identical filler with the
needle excised, five distinct document hashes.

**`n` is real `n`.** Every cell draws its needles from a disjoint slice of its class's combination
pool and asserts `duplicate_needles == 0`, so no cell's effective `n` is smaller than its nominal
`n`. Observed per cell: `n=200`, `duplicate_needles=0` (`plan.json: eval.cells`).

## 3. Leakage check — `experiments/h5-adapter/leakage_report.json`

Committed as code (`leakage_check.py`), re-derived from the **emitted pool files and the plan**, not
from the generator's own variables. 11 checks, 9 gating.

| # | check | result | gating |
|---|---|---|---|
| c1 | template **ids** disjoint train vs eval | 0 shared | yes |
| c2 | canonical template **sha256** disjoint | 0 shared | yes |
| c3 | **rendered needle sha256** disjoint, uniform *and* long-boost train vs eval | 0 shared | yes |
| c4 | longest shared **word** n-gram | **4** — `"i would like to"`, function words only | yes |
| c5 | longest shared **char** n-gram | 20 — spans a morphological variant (`appear` inside `appeared`) | **no, diagnostic** |
| c6 | max rendered token **Jaccard** over train × eval pairs | 0.267 | yes |
| c7 | slot **value** pools disjoint | 0 shared | yes |
| c8 | filler vs needle, whole content-word overlap + substring stems | **empty** | yes |
| c9 | label/position independence: every cell and regime balanced | all balanced; **oracle 0.2500** | yes |
| c10 | no template renders to nothing | none | yes |
| c11 | the needle prefix is absent from the filler | absent | yes |

**c5 is reported and is not gating, with the reason recorded in the artifact.** A shared character
window is not template reuse: the longest one here is a *substring* match across a morphological
variant. Word n-grams correctly do not treat that as a match, and the binding disjointness evidence
is the exact-hash checks c1–c3. The raw number is in the artifact so this reading can be checked
rather than taken on trust.

**The filler was authored for this experiment, and the reason is recorded.** `harness/pools/
filler-v1.json` was written against the earlier, narrower support-domain needle set and shares
generic content words (`team`, `quarter`, `window`, `run`, `desk`, `leaves`) with the wider H5
vocabulary. Rather than weaken the check, `pools/filler-h5-v1.json` was authored from 204 candidates
of which **104 were kept and 100 dropped**, with every drop and the words it hit listed in the pool
provenance. No sentence was rewritten to pass — candidates are only kept or dropped. The selection
rule is exactly the check's own criterion (`harness/pools.words` filtered by
`harness/pools.STOPWORDS`, plus the `forbidden_stems` substring test), so c8 now reports an **empty**
content-word overlap.

## 4. Evaluation protocol conformance

- `protocol.md` followed exactly; it was not modified.
- `n = 200` per cell, 50 per class; label counts reported beside every number.
- **random (0.25), majority class and the position-only oracle are reported for every cell.** On
  this item set the position-only oracle is **0.2500 exactly**, on every cell.
- Raw per-item predictions are written to `experiments/h5-adapter/predictions/*.jsonl`; every
  statistic is recomputed from them by `stats.py` using the program's canonical
  `experiments/harness/metrics.py` (its sha256 is recorded in `summary.json`).
- Percentile bootstrap CIs, 10 000 resamples, seeded. **McNemar exact** paired by item. Holm–Bonferroni
  across the family (2 candidates × 3 baselines × cells × seeds). ≥3 seeds, with the seed spread and
  a per-cell sign-agreement block reported.
- Option order permuted per item in `L4000-p100-perm`, so an answer read off the option *index* is
  separated from an answer read off the document.
- Environment, both git revisions, pool hashes and the exact command are in `manifest.json`.
- `max_len = 8192` everywhere; `head_max_len = 256` (the checkpoint default). Every row records
  `truncated` and `needle_kept`; the encoder cache is fp16, which is the dtype the checkpoint's own
  weights are and the dtype `Agent._infer` already computes in, so caching quantises nothing.

## 5b. A near-miss that would have produced a spurious refutation

**This is the most useful thing this experiment has produced so far, and it is a negative result
about the harness rather than about H5.**

A tiny randomly-initialised encoder has nothing to learn, so an end-to-end smoke test with one
cannot tell a working training loop from a broken one. `check_learnability.py` therefore drives the
**real** `train.train_arm` loop with a synthetic feature cache in the exact format
`FeatureStore` reads, whose state tokens literally carry the label — and a second cache whose state
carries an **unrelated** label, so "it learned" cannot mean "it memorised the item ids or fitted
the target-pairing bug". Artifact: `experiments/h5-adapter/learnability.json`.

Measured, `n = 256` synthetic items, `arm2_random_init`, 32 steps per epoch (so 60 epochs = 1920
steps and 250 epochs = 8000 steps):

| learning rate | epochs | steps | final **train** accuracy | final train loss |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1e-4** | 60 | 1 920 | **0.312** | 1.3832 |
| **1e-4** | 250 | 8 000 | **1.000** | 0.0004 |
| 5e-4 | 60 | 1 920 | **1.000** | 0.0004 |
| 2e-3 | 60 | 1 920 | **1.000** | 0.0000 |

**The learning rate originally chosen for the four-arm run was 1e-4 at 8 epochs — 2 016 optimizer
steps, essentially the 1 920-step row above.** At that budget the loop does not fit even a label
sitting directly in the features: 0.312 train accuracy against a 0.250 chance floor. The failure is
a **step-budget × learning-rate interaction, not a hard learning-rate ceiling** — the same 1e-4
reaches 1.000 given 8 000 steps, which is why the table reports both. Nothing here says 1e-4 is
unusable; it says 1e-4 was unusable *at the budget this experiment had chosen*.

Had the run gone ahead with it, all four arms would have underfitted identically, every arm would
have landed at chance, and the honest-looking conclusion would have been *"fine-tuning does not
help and neither does the architecture"* — a **spurious refutation of H5 produced entirely by a
hyper-parameter and a step budget**, with no bug anywhere for a verifier to find. The run now uses
`lr = 5e-4` at 12 epochs, chosen from **train** accuracy only; the evaluation set is never read by
the sweep, so this is not tuning on the test set.

Two further things it surfaced:

1. **The decision head carries no positional embedding of its own.** It is a transformer over the
   encoder's *contextualised* states, so it relies entirely on the encoder to make the option
   markers distinguishable. With every marker given the same input vector the four options are
   interchangeable and the softmax over them cannot beat `1/n_options` however well the label was
   read — a loss pinned at exactly `ln 4`. The synthetic cache now gives each marker a slot code,
   which is what RoPE provides in the real model. Worth stating because it is also a *mechanism*
   fact about the shipped head, not only about the test.
2. **The decoy arm is what makes the fit meaningful.** `highest_on_a_decoy_label` is reported beside
   `best_on_a_real_label` for each aggregation; a loop that fits both has learned nothing.

### 6.1 Per-cell accuracy, mean over seeds

Arm 1 is complete. Trained arms are still running at the time of writing; their columns fill from
the same artifact.

| cell | pad | needle pos | `n` | position-only oracle | **arm1 frozen** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `L0` | 0 | — | 200 | 0.25 | **0.640** |
| `L4000-p000` | 4000 | 0.00 | 200 | 0.25 | **0.580** |
| `L4000-p025` | 4000 | 0.25 | 200 | 0.25 | **0.295** |
| `L4000-p050` | 4000 | 0.50 | 200 | 0.25 | **0.335** |
| `L4000-p075` | 4000 | 0.75 | 200 | 0.25 | **0.280** |
| `L4000-p100` | 4000 | 1.00 | 200 | 0.25 | **0.415** |
| `L4000-p100-perm` | 4000 | 1.00, options reordered | 200 | 0.25 | **0.465** |
| `L4000-p100-ablated` | 4000 | needle removed | 200 | 0.25 | **0.250** |
| `L4000-p100-swapped` | 4000 | another class's needle | 200 | 0.25 | **0.250** |
| `L7000-p000` | 7000 | 0.00 | 200 | 0.25 | **0.595** |
| `L7000-p100` | 7000 | 1.00 (primary) | 200 | 0.25 | **0.365** |

Artifact: `experiments/h5-adapter/predictions/arm1_frozen.jsonl` (**measured**, n=200 per cell,
bootstrap CI half-width ±0.069 at this n). Reproduce with `stats.py`.

**Two things in that table matter more than the numbers.**

**The distance curve is U-shaped, not monotone.** On this item set the shipped head is *best when
the needle is adjacent to the decision block* (0.580 at position 0.00, 0.415 at position 1.00) and
*worst in the middle* (0.280–0.335 at positions 0.25–0.75). P1b measured only the two ends at
`pad=4000` (0.585 start, 0.300 end) and read them as a monotone decline; at `pad=4000` the start
figure reproduces here almost exactly (**0.580 vs P1b's 0.585**), but the interior was never
measured, and the interior sits *below* the end. A two-point sample cannot distinguish "accuracy
decays with distance" from "accuracy is high at both edges of the receptive field and low between
them", and the five-point sweep says it is the latter at this length. At `pad=7000` the two-point
picture does hold (0.595 → 0.365).

**The ablated and swapped controls collapse to a constant, and the collapse is the finding.** With
the needle removed, **all 200 predictions are `technical`**; accuracy is 0.250 only because the cell
is balanced 50 per class, not because the head behaves like a calibrated chance responder. With the
needle replaced by another class's, 195 of 200 predictions are still `technical` and
`follows_needle` is **0.275** — essentially the constant. So at this cell the shipped head's default
is a strong `technical` prior, and its above-chance 0.415 on the canonical cell comes from the
minority of items where it does read the evidence. Reporting "ablated = chance" without the
predicted distribution would have hidden that; `stats.py` reports the distribution beside every
control for exactly this reason.

An **oracle-free ceiling is not available from this table**, but the orchestrator's E-001 measured
~0.71 on the matched balanced pool, and arm 1 is at 0.640 even at `pad=0` — so the short-context
cell is not saturated either.

## 9. What this cannot claim

- Nothing about real long documents. The filler is synthetic and repetitive; repetition likely
  exaggerates attention dilution relative to natural text (`protocol.md` §10).
- Nothing about single-language transfer. This sweep is English-only; the multilingual checkpoint is
  used, but the sweep is not (`protocol.md` §2, §10).
- Nothing from a cell whose intervention was not asserted live. `liveness` is asserted by
  construction: `check_docs.py` proves the ablations and the option permutation change the prompt,
  and the arm's `head_kind` is recorded per run — a config patch that silently did nothing is
  reported as invalid, not as a null result.
- **This file does not declare a winner.** It reports numbers; `LEDGER.md` and a verifier assign
  confidence.
